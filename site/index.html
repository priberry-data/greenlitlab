Know it's safe
before you launch.

Greenlit looks at your app the way a stranger would. It finds leaked keys and open data, tells you in plain words what each one means, and writes the prompt that fixes it.

Free. No sign-up. Reads only what every visitor already sees.

Check. Fix. Check again.

  1. 01Check

    Paste your app's address. Usually in under thirty seconds, you get a grade from A to F and every problem a stranger could use.

  2. 02Fix

    Each problem comes with a prompt written for your builder. Paste them in one at a time, worst first.

  3. 03Check again

    Run it once the fix is live. The grade goes up, and you know the fix worked instead of hoping it did.

What it looks for

Only what Greenlit confirmed moves your grade. What it couldn't see, it says. What only you know, it asks.

Secret keys in your code
32 kinds, from Stripe, OpenAI, OpenRouter and AWS to Telegram bots and private keys, that anyone can copy from the JavaScript your site sends.
on the web
Your database's master key
A Supabase service_role or sb_secret_ key in the browser lets anyone read and change everything.
on the web
Your source code, handed out
Source maps that let anyone download the code behind your app.
on the web
Clickjacking and missing headers
The settings that stop other sites from framing your app or injecting scripts into it.
on the web
Leftovers that break a live app
A page still pointing at a developer's computer, payments in test mode, a test email sender, a default title, or a setting that hides you from Google.
on the web
Email people will trust
On your own domain: the records that stop others sending email as you, and keep your sign-up emails out of spam.
on the web
Data anyone can read
Supabase tables, storage buckets and Firebase data open to the whole internet. Counted, never read.

Sign-up that lets anyone in

Rules that let everyone in
Policies that check nothing, functions that skip sign-in, payment messages nobody verifies. Read from your project's files, or from Supabase's own advisor in plain words.

What only you know
Backups, spending limits, a separate test database. Seven questions, asked after every check. Never graded, because we can't verify your answers.

Run it where you build

Your own app, deeper

On your computer Greenlit can check your database too, because it can ask whether the app is yours.

$ npx greenlitlab myapp.lovable.app --mine
Inside your agent

Add it as an MCP server in Claude, Cursor or VS Code, and ask your agent to check what it just deployed.

$ npx -y greenlitlab mcp
In CI

Fail the build when something serious opens up after a change.

$ npx greenlitlab $APP_URL --fail-on high

We never sign in to your app, never read your users' data and never write to your database. The database checks run only on your own computer, when you say the app is yours. A check's result is kept for a few minutes and each site is rate-limited, to make it hard to use us to hammer a site. A public report exists only if you turn it on with a tag on your own app, and it comes down when you remove the tag. No cookies. No trackers. Open source, so you can check us too.
